## 1.0 Professional Leadership Project Overview

### What is the PLP?

The Professional Leadership Project (PLP) is the overarching project completed as part of the Executive Ready Program. It is an outcome-driven activity focused on:
- Leading positive change within your organisation
- Personal leadership growth
- Testing new strategies and methodologies
- Integrating learning into your everyday environment

### Key Steps

1. Collaborate with your Peer Coaching partner(s) on the project focus and how it aligns with your individual leadership growth
2. Collect data for the impetus for change and understand how the PLP can help enhance your organisation
3. Plan, monitor and lead change using a strong evidence base
4. Evaluate your progress
5. Celebrate success!

### Project Criteria

Your PLP must meet the following three criteria:

1. **Reflect, analyse, monitor and lead change** using a strong evidence base *(keeping a leadership diary throughout the program is recommended)*
2. **Utilise and apply your learning** from the program *(using a minimum of two models or leadership frameworks from the program or your independent leadership research)*
3. **Deliver a tangible outcome** in an area that you have identified that requires leadership development

### Project Topic/Focus

Key notes on project scope:
- The focus can be either **personal or professional**
- Your project could be work you are already completing, or something you will start during the program
- You do **not** need to complete the project within the timeframe of the program
- The aim is **not** to add substantially to your workload, but to apply new skills and strategies to your leadership practice

### Examples of Previous PLPs

**Organisational/Professional focus:**
- Create a team charter
- Create a staff PD to encourage a culture of trust and communication
- Introduce a new system and work through implementation with stakeholders
- Design/facilitate a workshop (or series) to up-skill staff
- Mentor junior staff with a focus on communication and confidence building
- Develop a new procedure and lead its implementation
- Spearhead the creation of a new initiative (e.g. Wellbeing initiative)
- Establish a support base for those struggling to adapt to new systems
- Create and implement an action plan to align team behaviours to organisational values
- Develop onboarding and training materials for new graduates/juniors
- Create a communication plan to improve communication between areas of the business
- Reformat and update company HR documents to align with new policies

**Personal leadership focus:**
- Improve time management and personal relationships in the workplace
- Develop a more diverse network including people from outside the workplace
- Implement new personal resilience strategies to improve mindfulness
- Focus on personal communication, EQ and monitoring interactions
- Build your personal and professional network
- Refine leadership purpose and align behaviours to purpose
- Build leadership capacity in providing feedback and being comfortable with the uncomfortable
- Learn to say no
- Focus on building time management skills and work/life balance
- Build reflective practice through daily journaling and using the Third Space

## PLP Assignment Overview

### Assignment 1: Framing your Professional Leadership Project
**Due:** One day prior to Webinar 4 (Module 4) | **Word count:** Maximum 1,000 words

Questions to address:
- Briefly describe your project. Describe the outcomes and benefits your project aims to achieve.
- Who are your key project stakeholders? Include your understanding of their needs, resistance, objections and how you could potentially address these.
- What are the potential barriers to the implementation of your project? How do you plan to overcome these?
- What learnings, strategies or models from the program do you plan to use to assist with your implementation?

### Presentation: Professional Leadership Project
**Due:** Workshop 3 (Day 3: in workshop) | **Time:** Maximum 5 minutes

The presentation catalogues your progress (successes and challenges) and asks you to reflect on your time spent on the PLP, as well as future pathways opened up as a result. Delivered to a small group of peers in a casual, supportive format.

Recommended aids: PowerPoint, visual aids, handouts.

Your presentation should cover:
- **Context:** What is your project, why did you choose it, who was involved?
- **Progress:** What steps have you taken towards your project?
- **Wins/Challenges:** Detail your successes and setbacks and how you overcame these
- **Reflections:** What stood out to you? Challenges overcome, relationships built, team dynamics, etc.
- **Next steps:** Where to from here?
- **How did I implement my learning?** What aspects of the Executive Ready program did you implement? How did learning from facilitators, your Peer Coach or fellow participants assist you?

### Assignment 2: Professional Leadership Project Reflective Report
**Due:** Four weeks after Webinar 6 (Reconnect) | **Word count:** Maximum 1,500 words

The report is constructed against the same questions as the PLP Presentation, and also incorporates learnings and feedback received during the presentation. You will consider the impact of your behaviours on the achievement of your goals, and ways to address, explore and overcome your own areas of resistance.

Report items mirror the presentation structure:
- Context
- Progress
- Wins/Challenges
- Reflections
- Next steps
- How did I implement my learning?

## 1.1 Problem Framing

### Core Concept

How we define (or frame) a problem influences the way we go about solving it.

Two key principles:
1. **Frame at the right level of abstraction** — be careful not to frame in terms of a solution. For example: *'What sort of car should I buy?'* vs *'How can I address my transportation need?'*
2. **Be mindful of your own biases** — get others involved with different perspectives

### How to Frame a Problem to Find the Right Solution
*(Adapted from Cantero-Gomez, 2019)*

- The 40-20-10-5 rule
- Research and collect information
- Rephrase and focus
- Challenge assumptions
- Broaden and narrow the view
- Change the perspective
- Frame questions, not statements
- Use positive language
- Become a problem maker
- Use the SCAMPER rule

### Principles of Sound Decision Making

- A good decision does not necessarily lead to a good outcome
- Decision making is often the final step in solving a problem
- Better decisions usually lead to better outcomes
- It is not always possible to know the best decision, but we can exercise judgement to make informed decisions
- Decision makers are subject to a wide range of factors that influence the quality of their decisions

### Problem Solving Traps

- Overconfidence
- Confirmation bias
- Ignoring new information
- Focusing on present needs rather than future ones
- Self-interest
- Mental modes

### Problem Framing Methods

**The W4 Method** *(30 minutes)*
Divide a whiteboard into four quadrants: Who, What, Why, Where. In 5 minutes, each person writes answers on sticky notes. Affinity diagram and dot vote the most important. Each participant then constructs a problem statement using the top-voted responses.

**Constraintstorming + Effort/Impact** *(60 minutes)*
State the main problem, then identify known constraints (time, budget, feasibility etc.). Affinity diagram and vote on the most important constraints. Assess whether constraints are flexible or inflexible. Place each problem on a 2x2 Effort/Impact grid.

**Pain Point Mapping** *(45 minutes)*
Document problems that are 'expensive', 'time consuming' or 'unnecessary/redundant'. Affinity diagram by category. Dot vote and ask stakeholders to assign a price/priority. Place stickies on a bullseye diagram — the closest to the centre is the target problem.

### Key Takeaway
> *"Falling in love with your problem, not your solution"* — the goal of problem framing is to deeply understand the right problem before moving to solutions.

## 1.3 Peer Coaching Session 2

### Appreciative Inquiry

Appreciative Inquiry (AI) is a coaching model first developed by David Cooperrider. It has been used around the world to create change in organisations, communities and with individuals. It is a way of telling a story with scaffolding — the way the story is enabled to be told is key.

**Core coaching skills emphasised:**
- Listening (not problem-solving)
- Asking questions with a curious mindset

### The Appreciative Inquiry Process — Five Steps

| Step | Coach's Role | Example Prompts |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Ask the respondent to think of her achievements | Be a good listener and celebrate achievements with them | *'Think back over your life so far… Think of times when it went really well for you… In a sentence, what were some of those times?'* |
| 2. Choose one time and tell it as a story | Keep the respondent talking about a time when she felt effective, happy and in the moment | *'Choose one of the times when you felt successful and effective, in control, living in the moment, happy… Tell me about it. What happened? How did it happen?'* |
| 3. Elicit the personal qualities that allowed them to be successful | Don't allow modesty. Encourage with persistence to tell about her own contributions to the success | *'What did it feel like? What was it about you and what you did that contributed to this success? What talents, skills and qualities did you show which allowed you to be successful?'* |
| 4. Take them into the future | Ask what they hope to achieve with those qualities. Encourage optimism | *'How could you use those talents, qualities, skills to help you to be what you want to be? …to make future changes in your life and your work?'* |
| 5. Bring them back to the present and near future | Ask how they will use their talents to achieve something valuable. Connect to the PLP | *'What are you going to achieve following this program? How can this align with your Professional Leadership Project? How can you use your talents and skills to achieve your PLP?'* |

### Key Principles for the Coach

- The number one skill as a coach is **listening**, not problem-solving
- Listen with a curious mindset
- Ask questions so the story can evolve
- Shine a light on strengths and personal qualities the coachee already possesses
- Facilitate awareness — do not provide answers
- Reflect back and focus on positive attributes
- Let the story be told

### Reflection After the Session

**Coach reflects on:**
- What worked?
- Where did I get stuck?
- What did I do in the moment, and what will I do in future?
- What could have been even better if…?

**Coachee reflects on:**
- What worked?
- What impact did the coaching conversation have on me?
- What did I leave the session with?
- What constructive feedback can I give my coach?

### Peer Coaching Session 2 Report

Following the coaching session, participants complete a reflective report and upload it to the Learning Portal. The report covers both the coaching role reflection and the coachee role reflection (as above).
