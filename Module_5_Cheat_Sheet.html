💡 5.1–5.2 Creativity & Innovation
Key Distinction
CreativityInnovation
Entirely new concept from inceptionNew application of established ideas
Original — nothing before itNew process, enactment, reconceptualisation
Innovation is less daunting — it's doing things differently, not from nothing
Culture Shapes Creativity (Shao et al., 2019)
  • Western: Novelty, originality, radical ideas, personal success
  • Eastern: Usefulness, social contribution, incremental change, tradition reinterpreted
  • Neither is superior — both are valid and valuable
Brainstorming Barriers in Diverse Teams
First idea wins Language fluency gap Saving face Big picture vs. detail
Key finding (Livermore, HBR): Diverse teams with low CQ produce less creative output than homogeneous teams.
Strategies for Inclusive Brainstorming
  1. Clearly define the objective — align everyone on the goal beforehand
  2. Give advance warning — allow prep time; offer written alternatives
  3. Insist on 100% participation — multiple formats (verbal, written, collective)
Barriers to Creativity (5.2)
External:Org culture, resource constraints, change fatigue, lack of buy-in
Internal:Fixed mindset, low intrinsic motivation, conflict aversion, self-doubt
🚧 5.5 Resistance to Change
Why Resistance Occurs
  • Sense of loss — familiar culture, role identity, ways of working
  • Lack of willingness — long-established habits are hard to shift
  • Lack of transparency — unclear communication or absent shared vision
  • Lack of skill — people fear they can't do what the change requires
Real Reasons People Resist (Jason Clarke)
  • Emotional overwhelm — too much fear to engage
  • Fear of the transition, not the idea itself
  • Uncertainty about how big the change really is
  • Not seeing how they fit into the change
  • Feeling they have no say
  • Scepticism from past experiences of fake change

The 8 Steps
1
Create urgency — honest dialogue about threats & opportunities
2
Guiding coalition — diverse, influential leaders; emotional commitment
3
Develop vision — clear, memorable, describable in 5 minutes
4
Communicate vision — embed in everything; walk the talk
5
Empower others — remove structural and human barriers
6
Short-term wins — celebrate early; use wins to build momentum
7
Consolidate gains — don't declare victory early; kaizen thinking
8
Anchor in culture — embed in hiring, training, succession
Getting Started on Innovation (5.3–5.4)
  1. Take the balcony view — step back, look holistically
  2. Create an opportunity list — what could be enhanced?
  3. Refine the list — what to move forward with?
  4. Anticipate challenges — whose mindset may not align?
  5. Choose the right time — consult widely, seek shared ownership
  6. Move incrementally — not radically
Timing matters: Avoid change fatigue — if the org is already in flux, staff may lack capacity for more.
🧠 5.7–5.8 Immunity to Change (Kegan & Lahey)
Core insight: The gap between intention and action is not about willpower or knowledge — it's a hidden psychological immune system protecting us from anxiety. (1 in 7 heart patients make the necessary changes even when told their life depends on it.)
The Five-Column Immunity Map
1
Improvement Goal
The change you want. Adaptive, behavioural, stated positively, about you.
e.g. "I am committed to backing myself and my abilities"
2
Doing / Not Doing Instead
Behaviours (conscious or unconscious) that sabotage your goal. Specific, honest, no justification.
e.g. "I browse the internet; I avoid the task with trivial work"
3
Hidden Competing Commitments
What you're actually committed to protecting. The "oh shit" feeling — what you fear if you did the opposite of col 2.
e.g. "I am committed to not being exposed as inadequate"
4
Big Assumptions
Unconscious beliefs treated as absolute truth. "If [col 3 situation], then something really bad will happen."
e.g. "I assume that if I back myself and fail, people will see I'm not capable"
5
Testing Strategies
SMART experiments: Safe · Modest · Actionable · Research-focused · Test. Small experiments to challenge whether col 4 is actually true.
e.g. "Back myself in one low-stakes meeting this week; observe what actually happens"
Three Mindset Blocks (Crowell)
  • "I can't do it" — fixed mindset. Counter: growth mindset — effort over time produces accomplishment
  • "People like me don't do this" — identity threat. Counter: find people with similar identity who do it successfully
  • "I don't actually want to" — extrinsic motivation only. Counter: build intrinsic interest and genuine connection to long-term hopes
Three Elements for Change to Stick
🔥 Gut:Fire in the belly — felt deeply, connected to people who matter
🧠 Head+Heart:Both reason and feeling must work together
✋ Hand:Intentional behavioural action — test the assumptions
"There's a big difference between deciding to do something and actually doing it." — Suzi Finkelstein

The Glass Cliff (Vongas & Al Hajj, 2015)
Glass CeilingGlass Cliff
Invisible barrier to reaching the topAppointed to declining orgs — high failure risk
  • "Think crisis — think female" heuristic drives appointments
  • Women preferred for intraorganisational crises (trust breakdown, internal conflict)
  • Men preferred for interorganisational crises (external threats, competitive warfare)
  • At extreme crisis intensity, stereotypically masculine traits become more desirable
  • Glass cliff perpetuates stereotypes by placing women in pre-set-up-to-fail roles
